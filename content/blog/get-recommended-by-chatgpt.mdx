You cannot pay ChatGPT to recommend you, and nobody can force a mention. What you can do is make your business clear, consistent and well covered online, so an assistant has good material to work with. These seven steps are the practical place to start.

## Why does ChatGPT name some businesses and not others?

ChatGPT builds an answer from what it has learned and, in some versions, from pages it finds while answering. A business that is described clearly in many trustworthy places is easier to name than one that appears once, in a vague way. OpenAI does not publish a ranking formula, so treat any "secret method" claim with suspicion. The steps below are sensible groundwork rather than tricks.

## Step 1: Ask the questions your customers ask

Before you change anything, see where you stand. Ask ChatGPT three or four questions in the way a customer would, for example:

- "Best [your service] in [your city]"
- "Which [your service] in [your city] is good for [a specific need]?"
- "Who should I call for [your service] near [your area]?"

Note who is named, in what order, and what reasons are given. Repeat in Gemini and Perplexity. The [free AI Visibility Check](/#check) automates this across five questions and three assistants. Our guide on [checking if AI recommends your business](/blog/check-if-ai-recommends-your-business) walks through the manual method.

## Step 2: Give every service its own clear page

Assistants find it easier to name you for something specific if your site has a page that says it plainly. Instead of one "Services" page, have a page for each main service. On each page, state what you do, who it is for, the city and areas you serve, and how to reach you. Write it the way you would explain it to a customer on the phone.

## Step 3: Make your facts identical everywhere

Check that your business name, address, phone number, opening hours and services read the same on:

- your website
- your Google Business Profile
- directories and listing sites in your category
- your social media profiles

Conflicting details, such as an old phone number on one site, make it harder for any tool to be sure of the facts. Google explains how to keep your profile accurate in its guide to [improving your local ranking](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en).

## Step 4: Answer real questions in plain words

Add short, direct answers to the questions customers ask most. Put the answer in the first two sentences, then add detail. A common structure is a question as a heading, a one-line answer, then a few lines of explanation. This helps people reading your page and gives assistants something clear to quote.

## Step 5: Find the sources AI trusts in your category

When an assistant names businesses, it often leans on a small set of sites: directories, review platforms, news pages or industry sites. Ask Perplexity your category question and look at the sources it lists, since it shows them. If a site appears again and again, make sure your business has a complete and accurate profile there.

## Step 6: Earn honest mentions and reviews

Reviews from real customers, mentions in local news, and listings on respected industry sites all add independent proof of what you do. Ask happy customers for honest reviews. Never buy fake reviews. Beyond being wrong, they put your reputation and your listings at risk.

## Step 7: Keep your content fresh and check again

Update pages when services, prices or hours change, and review them at least every few months. Then run the same questions again. Because answers vary between runs, look at the pattern over several weeks, not a single result.

## What does this look like for a local business?

Imagine a physiotherapy clinic in Bengaluru. This is an illustration, not a client result. Today it has one page called "Services", an old landline number on two directories, and no answers to common questions. An assistant asked for "the best physiotherapist in Indiranagar for back pain" has little clear material to use.

After the steps above, the clinic has a page for back pain treatment that names Indiranagar and explains who it helps. Its number and hours match on its website and on every listing. It has a short page answering "How many sessions will I need?" in plain words, without promising results. None of this guarantees a mention, but the assistant now has clear, consistent facts to draw on.

## How do you know if it is working?

Do not judge by one answer. Use the same five questions every month and record how many of the answers name you. Look for movement in three places:

1. **How often you are named.** Does the count of answers that name you rise over several months?
2. **Where you appear.** Are you moving from the end of a list towards the top?
3. **How correctly you are described.** Are the facts right, such as address, services and hours?

Small ups and downs between runs are normal. A steady trend over months is the signal that counts.

Keep a simple note each month: the date, the questions, how many answers named you, and one thing you changed since last time. After a few months, that note tells you which changes seemed to help and which did nothing, so you can spend your effort where it matters.

## What should you not do?

- **Do not stuff pages with keywords.** Assistants and people both prefer plain, helpful text.
- **Do not copy competitors' pages.** Copied text adds nothing new and can hurt you.
- **Do not trust promises of guaranteed mentions.** Nobody outside the AI companies controls the answers.
